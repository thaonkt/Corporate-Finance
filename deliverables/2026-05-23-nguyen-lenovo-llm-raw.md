## 1. Executive Summary

Lenovo enters FY2025 in measurably better shape than twelve months prior,
but the underlying structure remains fragile. Three findings define this
assessment.

First, the business mix shift is beginning to register in the income
statement. Net margin expanded from 1.94% (FY2024) to 2.12% (FY2025),
driven by SSG's 21.1% operating margin and ISG's return to profitability
— but the improvement is modest against an asset-light distribution base
generating USD 69.1B in revenue. Consolidated margins remain structurally
thin because the Intelligent Devices Group, which still represents 53% of
revenue, operates at sub-2% segment margins and dilutes every dollar of
high-margin SSG revenue.

Second, leverage remains the dominant ROE driver and the primary risk
amplifier. The equity multiplier of 6.51x accounts for the majority of
Lenovo's 22.95% ROE — not margin expansion or efficiency gains. Times
Interest Earned of 2.80x sits uncomfortably below the 3.0x
covenant-buffer threshold, and a 20x spike in short-term borrowings
(USD 50M → USD 1,031M) coincident with a 45% operating cash flow decline
creates a liquidity pinch that warrants active management.

Third, efficiency is the most unambiguous positive in FY2025. Asset
turnover improved from 1.47x to 1.66x; DSO contracted from 52.3 to 49.3
days; DIO fell from 52.0 to 46.0 days — all pointing to disciplined
working capital management even as revenue grew 21%. The strategic
question for FY2026 is whether ISG's higher capital intensity will reverse
these gains as the segment scales.

## 3. Profitability Ratios

*ROA FY2025 uses average assets (USD 41.5B). ROA FY2024 uses year-end
assets (no prior-prior data). ROC denominator: LTD + total equity.*

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change | Interpretation |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Net Profit Margin | 1.94% | 2.12% | +0.18pp | Thin but improving; IDG volume drag persists |
| ROA | 2.84% | 3.52% | +0.68pp | Below Dell ~8%, HP ~10%; structural gap |
| ROC | N/A | 19.43% | — | Strong pre-financing return; leverage is the issue |
| ROE | 18.12% | 22.95% | +4.83pp | Multiplier-driven; see Du Pont section |

ROA's structural deficit versus Dell and HP is not primarily a management
failure — it reflects Lenovo's deliberately asset-light distribution model.
Dell and HP own far less inventory and receivables relative to revenue
because they outsource more manufacturing; Lenovo retains more of the
supply chain, inflating the asset base and compressing ROA. The more
meaningful signal is ROA's +68 bps improvement year-on-year, driven by
21% revenue growth with only 14% asset growth — a genuine efficiency gain.

ROE at 22.95% appears respectable against a cost of equity of approximately
8.6%. The critical caveat: 6.51x equity multiplication is doing most of
the work. Net margin (2.12%) and asset turnover (1.66x) together produce
only 3.52% ROA — the remaining 19.43 percentage points of ROE is pure
leverage effect. This makes Lenovo's ROE inherently fragile: a 1 percentage
point deterioration in net margin would reduce ROE by approximately 6.5
percentage points.

## 5. Leverage Ratios

*Total debt ratio = total liabilities / total assets. FY2024 TIE based
on approximate actual EBIT of USD 1,069M.*

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change | Interpretation |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Total Debt Ratio | 84.31% | 84.94% | +0.63pp | Stable but high; driven by payables float |
| TIE | 1.40x | 2.80x | +1.40x | Doubled YoY; still below 3.0x comfort threshold |
| Debt Burden | 28.6% | 64.3% | +35.7pp | Dramatic improvement from FY2024 stress |

The total debt ratio of 84.94% demands context. Lenovo operates a negative
working capital business model: accounts payable (USD 11.98B) and other
current liabilities (USD 16.98B) together account for USD 28.96B of the
USD 37.57B total liabilities — roughly 77%. Strip out trade payables and
accruals, and Lenovo's financial debt ratio drops to approximately 15%
of assets.

TIE of 2.80x is the genuine concern. While the year-on-year improvement
from 1.40x is dramatic — reflecting EBIT's near-doubling as ISG moved
from loss to profit — the absolute level remains below the 3.0x threshold.

Short-Term Debt Spike: Short-term borrowings increased 20.4x from
USD 50.4M (FY2024) to USD 1,031M (FY2025). This coincides with the
issuance of the USD 2B convertible bond in FY2025, suggesting a portion
was temporarily held in short-dated instruments. The spike is most likely
transactional rather than structural.

## 8. Strategic Recommendations

**Recommendation 1 — Strengthen Interest Coverage Before Next Debt Maturity**
Evidence: TIE improved from 1.40x (FY2024) to 2.80x (FY2025). Still below
3.0x. Short-term borrowings spiked 20x to USD 1,031M.
Action: Target TIE ≥ 3.5x by FY2027. Retire short-term debt spike within
12 months. Restrict convertible issuance until EBIT reaches USD 2.8B.

**Recommendation 2 — Accelerate SSG Attach Rate to Drive Margin Above 3%**
Evidence: Net margin 2.12% vs. Dell 3.5%, HP 6.0%. SSG 21.1% margin but
only 15–18% of revenue. Every 1pp mix shift adds ~0.19pp consolidated margin.
Action: Target SSG at 22% of revenue by FY2027. Mandate SSG bundling in
all IDG enterprise deals above USD 100K.

**Recommendation 3 — Protect Asset Turnover as ISG Scales**
Evidence: Asset turnover improved 1.47x → 1.66x. ISG capital intensity
threatens compression toward 1.55x by FY2027.
Action: Capital efficiency gate for ISG investments above USD 50M: projected
asset-turn contribution ≥ 1.2x within 24 months.

**Recommendation 4 — Rebuild OCF to USD 2B+ by FY2027**
Evidence: OCF declined 45.3%. OCF minus interest leaves only USD 327M
before dividends of USD 608M — structural shortfall.
Action: Milestone-based billing on ISG contracts above USD 10M. Net-30
payment terms with early payment incentive.
